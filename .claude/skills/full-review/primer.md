# RMG code review: primer for every reviewer

## The project

RMG (`/home/glyge/Projects/sw/rmg`) is a random music generator in C# .NET 10 with a web page:

- `Rmg.Core`: the generator. It makes a song (MIDI) from a seed and settings.
- `Rmg.WebApi`: an ASP.NET server, which serves songs, analytics and an admin dashboard. `Rmg.WebApi/Client/` is the browser page, plain ES modules.
- `Rmg`: a CLI.
- `Rmg.Tests`: TUnit tests. `Rmg.Tests.Page`: Playwright tests of the page.

**Read `/home/glyge/Projects/sw/rmg/CLAUDE.md` first.** Its principles are the project's rules, and a breach of one is a finding.

**Read `decided.md` (beside this primer) too.** It lists findings that earlier reviews raised and the project decided against or kept on purpose, with the reasons. Do not raise one again unless the code it names has changed since in a way that undoes the reason; then say so. `ROADMAP.md`'s *Decided, and kept* and *Parked* sections are decisions of the same kind.

## How a song is made

1. **Decisions.** `SongDecisions` draws the song-level decisions: form, meter, tempo, genre, parts, sections. Each section is generated by `SectionGenerator` as layered state (song, section, track, bar, note `StateMap`s), not as notes.
2. **Assembly.** `SongFormGenerator` places sections, the intro and the ending into a `SongMap`.
3. **Stages.** `SongStages.All` runs the ordered stages listed in the review folder's `stages.txt`. Each takes `(SongDecisions, SongDraft)` and returns a `SongDraft`.
   - Stages before "Notes" edit state. "Notes" is `Realizer`, which chooses pitches and registers.
   - Stages after "Notes" edit `RealizedNote`s.
4. **Rendering.** `Render` turns the song into a `RenderedSong`. Velocity there is a sum mapped through a tanh curve (`Render.ToMidiVelocity`), so scaling a velocity sum by a factor does not scale loudness: `Render.ScaleLoudness` scales it on the heard scale. `Midi.cs` writes the file.

### The map
`SongMap` places the sections, each a `SectionSpan` in a meter of its own (`Meter`, its bar's groups, its phrase's bars `PhraseBars`, its pattern's `Phrases`), whose bars count from the section's start, not the song's; a section's last bar may be cut short (`SectionSpan.Cut`, `ShortBars`), and `SongMap.MeterAt` gives the short bar's own meter there. A position measured against the wrong origin or meter is a class of bug this code has had, so the map answers it in one place: `SongMap.PlaceOf` (a position's section, bar, beat and pattern bar), a span's `Bars`, `Phrases` and `Patterns` (each walked from the section's start, the short last one its own length) and `Span.Within` (one tolerance). A bar, phrase or pattern walked or counted by hand (`Math.Floor(position / BarDuration)`, a loop adding `BarDuration`) is a finding where the map has the answer.

### What plays where
`SongMap.PlayAt` gives the appearance a position plays as: an intro made from a section plays as that section, a hook intro as its chorus, the ending as the last section. A stage that reads a section's decisions at a position asks it, never the section the position merely falls in.

### What the song holds
`SongDraft.Holds(song)` lists the stretches the song holds (`HeldSpan`, by kind `Hold`: a fill, stop time as played, a drum solo, a part cut by the form, a call and its answer), and a stage keeps out of the kinds it names (`Holds.Meets`, `At`, `NextAfter`). A stage that makes such a stretch writes it there; a stage keeping out of a stretch by a list of its own, or one making a stretch others should keep out of without writing it, is a finding.

### Conventionality
`Unconventionality` runs from 0 (plainest) to 1 (wildest), per facet: Feel, Groove, Chords, Progression, Melody, Form, Sound, Fills, Scale.
- Choices lean through `ByConvention(plain, tuned, wild)` and `Tilt`.
- A genre (`Genre`, from `Client/genres.json`, choices declared in `GenreChoices`) overrides options.
- The mood (`Mood`): darkness and intensity, each from -1 to 1, drawn within a genre's span or given, beside the unconventionality. It never adds or removes an option: it leans the weights of those a genre's pool allows, by each option's lean in `Mood.Leans`, as energy leans. A choice the mood should lean and `Mood.Leans` does not name, or a lean applied by hand elsewhere, is a finding.
- Figures (`Figures`) are data: a player's hand-written figure (four on the floor, the skank, the tresillo, the waltz, the boom-chicka, the jazz ride, the bass on the kick) with its genres' amounts and its energy lean; a figure written as a case in a stage, where the table would hold it, is a finding.

### Streams
Every decision draws from its own seeded stream (`SongStream`, `SectionStream`, `Seeds.Derive`). Adding a draw to an existing stream changes every later draw, so the corpus fingerprint (`CorpusFingerprintTest`) guards against that.

### Tests
- **Rules:** assert what holds of every song.
- **Calibrations:** keep wide bounds and print what they measure.
- **Explicit `Report` tests:** print measurements and run only when asked.
- Tests read the trace's typed values (`StateTrace`, `TracePoints`), never its text descriptions.
- The corpus can be moved onto other songs (`RMG_CORPUS_OFFSET`), so a test must hold for any songs: a part, a device or a section kind a test assumes every song has is a finding.

### Comment style
Doc comments are dense English prose describing the music. The style is deliberate. Flag a comment only where it is wrong or stale, not for its style.

## Your checklist, for every file in your slice

1. **Correctness:** logic errors, off-by-one, wrong units (beats against seconds, steps against semitones), positions counted from the wrong origin or in the wrong meter (the song's start against the section's, a full bar against a short one, four bars against a phrase of another length), null and empty cases, crashes, wrong order of operations, state leaking between songs, thread safety where code runs in parallel.
2. **Principles (CLAUDE.md):**
   - Streams: a new draw needs its own stream, or keeps the draw count.
   - Chances lean via `ByConvention`/`Tilt`, never multiplied or capped by hand.
   - No silent defaults in wiring.
   - Ask a track's role, never its number.
   - State decides, `Realizer` realizes.
   - A stage reads only what earlier stages made.
   - Bars from the map, what plays where by `PlayAt`, keep-outs by `Holds` (see above).
3. **Overengineering:** code that could be cut, merged with a sibling mechanism, made data, or simplified. Duplicated logic.
4. **Optimization:**
   - Hot paths in generation and rendering: per-note or per-bar loops doing O(n) scans inside O(n) loops, repeated LINQ re-enumeration, repeated allocation, `ToArray`/`ToList` in loops, repeated `GetEffectiveStateMapAt` or `Where(...).Any` on whole timelines, recomputing what could be cached.
   - Estimate the cost: how often the code runs, per song or per note. Ignore one-offs.
5. **Musicality:** a rule that would make something sound wrong, or what a real player or producer would do differently. Be concrete. And how the parts sound together, which no rule of a note against its own chord sees; `run/sounding.txt` measures each preset's:
   - a chord's note taken from the chord's own notes (`Realizer.GetChordNotes`), never a fixed scale step that assumes a triad: a sus, power or shell chord has no third, and a step of 2 above its root plays the third it leaves out (a bass's inversions did, 13% of Chamber's bass over a sus4);
   - a line copied or moved in time (a doubling, an imitation, a quote, a polymeter's copy, a hook intro) judged against what sounds where it lands, the line it copies playing on there too, not only against the chord there (an imitation's notes a bar late rubbed against the melody, 16-18% of them);
   - a note's length against its part's next note, on an instrument that does not ring on: one held into the next overlaps itself (chords struck in 16ths kept their source chord's length, their cycles running together);
   - anything reading the chord, the key or the harmony at a time, over a span no pitched part plays in (a drums' intro, a break, stop time): what it reads there.
6. **Dead code and stale docs:** unused members, parameters always passed the same value, comments that no longer describe the code.
7. **Tests** (when your slice is tests):
   - A test that holds only for the corpus's own songs (see *Tests* above).
   - Rules asserted as rules, calibrations bounded widely.
   - A test that can pass vacuously, for example a loop over zero items followed by no count assert.
   - Assertions on description strings.
   - Duplicated tests, slow tests, flaky patterns.
   - Tests that do not test what their name says.
   - Gaps: an important behaviour in the code under test that nothing asserts.
8. **Client and server** (when your slice is web code): security, input validation, error handling, races, the contract with the other side, accessibility, memory leaks, event listeners never removed.

## How to work

- **Read every line of every file in your slice.** Use Read with offsets for long files. The point of this review is full coverage, not sampling. Do not stop early.
- You may open other files to understand a call (Read and Grep are allowed), but report findings only in your slice's files. If you notice something elsewhere, put it under `elsewhere` with its file and line.
- **Read only. Never edit any file in the repository, never build, never run tests or `dotnet`.**
- Be concrete and verified. Every finding needs:
  - the exact file and line;
  - the code quoted;
  - why it is wrong;
  - a failure scenario: inputs or state, and the wrong outcome.
- No speculation without saying so. Prefer fewer, solid findings over many weak ones. Style nits are not findings.
- Severity:
  - `high`: a crash, wrong music in many songs, a security hole, a principle breach with real effect.
  - `medium`: a real bug in a corner, a meaningful inefficiency, notable overengineering.
  - `low`: minor.

## Output

Write one JSON file, the path given in your task, with this shape:

```json
{
  "slice": "<id>",
  "coverage": [{"file": "<repo-relative path>", "lines": 123, "read": "full" | "partial", "note": "<if partial, why>"}],
  "findings": [
    {
      "file": "<repo-relative path>",
      "line": 42,
      "category": "correctness|principles|overengineering|optimization|musicality|dead-code|stale-doc|tests|security|web|genre-fit|cut|idiom-gap|architecture|foundation",
      "severity": "high|medium|low",
      "summary": "<one sentence>",
      "evidence": "<the code quoted, a few lines>",
      "scenario": "<concrete inputs or state, and the wrong outcome>",
      "suggestion": "<the fix, briefly>",
      "confidence": "certain|likely|possible",
      "kind": "mistake|taste",
      "listen": {"question": "<what a listener is asked>", "genre": "<a preset id, or none>", "without": "<the patch or stage left out that makes the song without>", "rank": 1}
    }
  ],
  "elsewhere": [{"file": "...", "line": 0, "summary": "..."}],
  "slice_notes": "<two or three sentences on the slice as a whole: its design, what is good, what is weak>"
}
```

`kind` and `listen` go on `musicality`, `genre-fit` and `cut` findings alone (`cut`, `idiom-gap`, `architecture` and
`foundation` are the musical, architecture and foundations reviewers' own, as `angles.md` says). A **mistake** is wrong by a rule the code or the
music states: notes against the chord, a device playing where its doc says it does not, a plain end playing a wild
thing. A **taste** is a judgement of how it sounds, which no reviewer can hear: it is fixed only after a listener
hears it, so give it a `listen` the page's listening mode could ask (a genre where the device plays, the question, what
the song without it leaves out, and its rank among your taste findings, 1 the one whose answer would change most songs).
No verifier checks a taste finding, and listening has kept most of what reviewers judged by reading, so propose few: a
slice or angle reviewer at most two, the musical reviewers as `angles.md` says.

Validate that the file is valid JSON. Your final message is one line: the path written, and the counts of findings by severity.
